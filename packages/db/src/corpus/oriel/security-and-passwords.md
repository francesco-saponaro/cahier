# Security and passwords

Oriel runs charging hubs on public streets and holds the badge data of thousands of couriers, so a security mistake is a safety and a privacy problem, not only an IT one. These rules are short on purpose.

## Accounts

One account per person, no shared logins, ever. Every account uses the single sign-on and a hardware key or the authenticator app as the second factor; SMS is not accepted. Service accounts belong to a team, are named for what they do, and have an owner in the inventory. An account that has not been used for 90 days is disabled, and after 180 days it is deleted.

## Passwords

Where a password still exists, it is at least 14 characters, generated by the password manager, and never reused. The company password manager is mandatory for every work secret; a secret in a message, a document or a spreadsheet is a security incident and is rotated the same day. Passwords are never sent by email, and nobody at Oriel will ever ask you for yours.

## Devices

Laptops are encrypted, lock after five minutes, and run the management agent that applies updates within seven days of release. Personal devices do not hold company data, with one exception: the chat and mail apps on a phone enrolled in the management tool. A lost or stolen device is reported to IT within one hour, at any time of day; IT wipes it remotely and the loss is logged, without blame.

## Access

Access is granted to a role, not a person, and reviewed every quarter by the team lead. Production access to the hub fleet is limited to the on-call rotation and the firmware team, through the bastion, with every session recorded. Customer data is read through the support tool, never exported, and never pasted into a chat or an outside tool.

## Phishing and reporting

Report anything suspicious with the "report" button in the mail client or on the security channel. A report that turns out to be nothing is still the right call. Clicking a bad link is not a disciplinary matter; hiding that you clicked is. The security team runs a test phishing campaign each quarter and shares the results by team, never by name.

## Secrets in code

Secrets live in the secrets manager and reach the services as environment variables at start-up. A secret committed to a repository is rotated within the hour, even on a private branch, because history is forever. The pre-commit hook scans for keys; do not bypass it.

## Incidents

A security incident is anything that may have exposed data or let someone in: a lost device, a leaked secret, a phishing click with credentials entered, a strange login. Call the on-call engineer; the security lead decides within an hour whether it is a reportable breach, which under the GDPR means notifying the regulator within 72 hours. Customers are told plainly, by the account owner, with what happened and what is being done.
